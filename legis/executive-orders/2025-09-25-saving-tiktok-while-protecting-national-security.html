SAVING TIKTOK WHILE PROTECTING NATIONAL SECURITY ​

Listen to the summary:

Published on Thursday, September 25, 2025

  • The Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act regulates applications operated by TikTok and ByteDance based on national security concerns.
  • Section 2(a) prohibits distribution, maintenance, or updating of certain foreign adversary controlled applications within the U.S.:
    • Entities cannot provide services for these applications through app stores or other marketplaces.
    • Effective date of prohibitions is January 19, 2025, with several delays through executive orders until December 16, 2025.
  • Section 2(c) allows the President to determine a "qualified divestiture" of TikTok, which would remove prohibitions under the Act if TikTok's control passes from foreign adversaries to U.S. interests.
  • A Framework Agreement proposes:
    • TikTok’s U.S. operations will be run by a new U.S.-based joint venture, owned 80% by U.S. entities.
    • ByteDance Ltd. and its affiliates will own less than 20%.
    • New governance and data protection measures will enhance national security.
  • The proposed divestiture is seen as a resolution to national security concerns while allowing American users to continue using TikTok.
  • The interagency process, led by the Vice President and various national security and law enforcement agencies, reviewed the divestiture plan.
  • Findings from this process include:
    • TikTok is a significant social media platform for approximately 170 million Americans.
    • The divestiture alleviates concerns about foreign control.
    • Strict controls on algorithms, data storage within U.S. infrastructures, and ongoing monitoring by trusted U.S. partners will take place.
  • The Attorney General is directed not to enforce the Act for 120 days post-order to facilitate the divestiture process.
  • The order revokes a previous presidential memorandum and modifies existing orders related to ByteDance and TikTok.
  • The President retains the authority to issue further orders necessary for national security.
  • The order clarifies that it does not create enforceable rights against the U.S. government or its entities.
