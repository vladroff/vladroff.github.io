MAKING FEDERAL ARCHITECTURE BEAUTIFUL AGAIN ​

Listen to the summary:

Published on Thursday, August 28, 2025

  • Objective of the Order: Update policies guiding Federal architecture to ensure buildings inspire civic virtue and serve the American people effectively.

  • Historical Context:

    • Emphasizes the Founders' intent for Federal buildings to reflect classical architecture and connect to democratic ideals.
    • Notes the shift in architecture from classical to modern and brutalist styles in the 1960s, which faced public criticism.

  • Design Policy:

    • Federal public buildings should:
      • Uplift and beautify public spaces.
      • Inspire citizens and be visually recognizable as civic structures.
      • Respect regional architectural heritage, with classical architecture as the preferred style.
    • Care must be taken when diverging from preferred styles to ensure designs embody American values.

  • Definition of Terms:

    • Applicable Federal public buildings: Includes courthouses, agency headquarters, and buildings over $50 million, excluding certain projects.
    • Architectural Styles: Defines classical, brutalist, and deconstructivist architecture.

  • Guiding Principles for Federal Architecture:

    • Emphasize dignity, stability, and regional reflection in design.
    • Designs should prioritize the needs and aspirations of the American public.
    • Necessary facilities should be designed with architectural excellence in mind.

  • General Services Administration (GSA) Directives:

    • GSA to update policies according to this order.
    • Ensure GSA architects have training or experience in classical architecture.
    • Establish a senior advisor position specializing in classical design.
    • Encourage participation from classical architecture firms in design competitions.
    • Require Presidential notification for designs that diverge from preferred architecture.

  • Limitations and Implementations:

    • The order does not diminish the authority of executive departments.
    • Must be implemented consistent with applicable law and budget availability.
    • Does not create enforceable rights against the U.S. government.
