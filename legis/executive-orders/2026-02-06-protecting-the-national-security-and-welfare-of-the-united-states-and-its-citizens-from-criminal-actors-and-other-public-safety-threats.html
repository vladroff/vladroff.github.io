PROTECTING THE NATIONAL SECURITY AND WELFARE OF THE UNITED STATES AND ITS CITIZENS FROM CRIMINAL ACTORS AND OTHER PUBLIC SAFETY THREATS ​

Listen to the summary:

Published on Friday, February 6, 2026

Headline: U.S. boosts criminal record sharing for border security.

Why it matters: The order aims to improve U.S. border safety by allowing immigration authorities greater access to criminal records, and by expanding information-sharing with allied nations to screen potential travelers and immigrants.

What the order does:

  • Directs the Department of Justice to give Homeland Security access to federal criminal records for screening and vetting.
  • Authorizes Homeland Security to share criminal history info with trusted foreign partners, under agreements with privacy safeguards.
  • Limits foreign sharing to reciprocal arrangements for screening travelers and immigrants.

Who it affects:

  • Non-U.S. nationals applying to enter or stay in the U.S. or partner countries.
  • U.S. immigration and border officials.
  • Allied border security and immigration authorities.

Practical impact on a regular person:

  • Foreign travelers or immigrants with criminal histories face stricter scrutiny.
  • Americans’ privacy is protected by safeguards in international data exchanges.

Context: DHS sharing is limited to partners with formal security agreements and reciprocity in place.
