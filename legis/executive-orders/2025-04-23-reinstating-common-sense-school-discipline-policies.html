REINSTATING COMMON SENSE SCHOOL DISCIPLINE POLICIES ​

Listen to the summary:
education
Department of EducationDepartment of JusticeDepartment of DefenseDepartment of Health and Human ServicesDepartment of Homeland SecurityOffice of Management and Budget

Published on Wednesday, April 23, 2025

  • The Federal Government will no longer tolerate risks to children's safety in classrooms from school discipline based on discriminatory “equity” ideology.
  • The 2014 "Dear Colleague" letter, which prompted schools to adopt race-based discipline practices, is criticized for making schools less safe.
  • A 2018 report noted that schools avoided disciplining misconduct to prevent racial disparity in discipline statistics, leading to increased classroom disorder.
  • Disciplinary decisions should focus on individual student behavior, not racial statistics, as concluded by the Federal Commission on School Safety.
  • The 2014 letter was rescinded, but 2023 guidance reintroduced race-based disciplinary practices, causing more classroom disorder.

Actions Ordered:

  • 30 Days: Secretary of Education to issue new guidance on school discipline to prevent racial discrimination under Title VI.
  • Immediate Action: Secretary of Education to act against institutions not complying with Title VI protections.
  • 60 Days: Coordination with Governors and State Attorneys General on preventing racial discrimination in school discipline.
  • 90 Days: Secretary of Defense to issue a revised discipline code for military-service families.
  • 120 Days: Secretary of Education to submit a report including:
    • Analysis of past Title VI investigations.
    • Assessment of non-profits promoting discriminatory discipline and recommendations.
    • Evaluation of alternative discipline policies.
    • Model policies promoting safety and American values, free from discrimination.

Notes:

  • The order does not limit the authority of executive departments or agencies.
  • Implementation is subject to applicable law and funding availability.
  • The order does not create enforceable rights against the U.S. or its entities.
